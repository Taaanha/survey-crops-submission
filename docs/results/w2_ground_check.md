# W2 satellite vs ground check: rain against BMD monthly normals

Generated by `scripts/build_w2_reports.py`. Do not edit by hand; re-run the script.

Ground: Bangladesh Meteorological Department, Average Normal Rainfall in mm (station monthly normals), https://server8.bmd.gov.bd/file/2016/08/17/pdf/53.pdf (stations Comilla, Feni, Maijdi Court = Noakhali, Sylhet; no Brahmanbaria station in the table). Satellite: NASA GPM IMERG V07 Final Run daily (`GPM_3IMERGDF.07`, 0.1 degree cell at the district point) and NASA POWER daily `PRECTOTCORR` (about 0.5 degree), monthly means of 2001-2024 (24 years, every day of a month observed). Bias = satellite minus BMD normal; percent is of the normal.

## Caveats (read before using the numbers)

- **The BMD table does not state its averaging period**, so the normal and our 2001-2024 mean may cover different years. A difference can be climate change or natural swings, not only satellite error.
- A station is a point; IMERG is a 0.1 degree cell (about 11 km) and POWER about 0.5 degree. The district point we use is not the station site, so some of the bias is distance.
- Four stations is a small check, not a validation of every district. This says nothing about Brahmanbaria.
- Monthly means only: daily accuracy and heavy-rain days are not checked here.

## Summary

- IMERG annual totals are more than 10% below the BMD normal at 3 of 4 stations (Feni, Maijdi Court (Noakhali), Sylhet); Comilla is 7.8%.
- By mean absolute monthly bias, IMERG is closer to BMD than POWER at 0 of 4 stations. **On this check the satellite is not better than POWER on bias.**
- This does not reverse the choice of IMERG as the rain source: POWER rain has level shifts around 2014-15 (`w2_homogeneity.md`, T9b), so its 2001-2024 mean mixes a low early level with a high late level and can land near a normal by offset, not by being right. A steady bias like IMERG's low monsoon months is easier to state than a jump.
- Because IMERG tends to read low against these normals, rain totals and dry-spell counts from IMERG should be read as estimates from NASA data for the area around the point, not as station rain.
